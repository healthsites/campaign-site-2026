Our Role in the DRC Ebola Response

On 15 May 2026, the DRC's Ministry of Public Health declared the country's 17th Ebola outbreak — the largest Bundibugyo virus (BDBV) outbreak ever recorded, spreading across Ituri, Nord Kivu, and Sud Kivu with confirmed cross-border transmission into Uganda. WHO declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern two days later; Africa CDC followed with a continental-level emergency declaration the day after.

Within weeks, DRC's INRB and INOHA, working with INSP and a consortium including Africa CDC, WHO, Northeastern University, the University of Oxford, and Flowminder, had a live Epidemic Intelligence Dashboard running to support the response. Alongside GRID3's official facility dataset, the dashboard carries healthsites.io's crowdsourced, OpenStreetMap-derived layer of health facility locations.

GRID3's facility data is authoritative but periodically refreshed; healthsites.io's OSM-based layer is continuously editable by the mapping community, so it can pick up a newly stood-up MSF Ebola Treatment Centre faster than a static survey can. That facility data feeds directly into core dashboard functions: which zones have testing and treatment capacity, travel time from suspected-case locations to the nearest functioning facility, and the dashboard's spatial risk model estimating each health zone's probability of outbreak invasion.

healthsites.io participates in Missing Maps as the Global Healthsites Mapping Project, alongside founding members the Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT), Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) UK, the British Red Cross Society, and the American Red Cross, and close to twenty other organizations — all mapping into the same shared OpenStreetMap base that MSF's Ebola Treatment Centres and isolation units get added to as they're stood up.

The consortium published the dashboard as a research letter in The Lancet Infectious Diseases — a case for real-time, locally led epidemic intelligence, run by the institutions closest to the outbreak.

Read the study in The Lancet Infectious Diseases →

Every data point carries a sourced, auditable trail. The Ministry of Health stays the validator of record for its own national data. No personal data is collected, only facility metadata. The data is licensed under ODbL, so it stays a public good rather than becoming anyone's proprietary asset. This baseline data wasn't collected for this outbreak — it's cumulative, community-maintained, and simply there, ready, the moment a response needs it.

Beyond this response, we are building a data collaborative in the DRC — a shared, permanent home for facility data across responders, ministries, and research partners. We are inviting support to implement the full data pipeline behind it.
